# V5.1 difficulty and progression contract

The first two authored boards remain tutorial stages. `level-3` is the existing engine adapter for the certified Piachan challenge collection; it is not a difficulty tier. A puzzle's stable `puzzleId` and `boardHash` identify its board independently of its position in the selector. `seed` and generator version are provenance, not a difficulty rating or unlock condition. No bank record, geometry, certificate, or recognition rule is changed by this milestone.

The neutral `certified-piachan-v1` collection contains the 105 existing IDs. Challenge numbers are display positions only; the game state always stores the certified ID. Retry reloads that ID; New Puzzle advances to a different certified ID. Both tutorials remain directly accessible, and finishing tutorial 2 enters the challenge collection. There is no difficulty-based gate until human playtests validate one. Future 45-, 60-, or 75-tile layouts require their own certified geometry and board records; no such layouts are shipped here.

## Offline diagnostics

Run `node tools/runDifficultyEvaluation.mjs` from the repository root to regenerate `tools/difficulty-report.json`. The report is keyed by exact ID and board hash. It measures tile count, layers, initially exposed tiles, whole-board feasible recognized compositions, characters shared by multiple feasible compositions, composition pairs sharing characters, verified-witness tray occupancy/pressure, and legal-action counts at three witness checkpoints. Whole-board feasibility is an upper bound, not a claim that every composition can be safely played. Witness measurements establish existence of a path, not human ease.

Four deterministic policies each run with the report's fixed seed and 140-action budget: immediate composition, random legal action, tray-capacity-aware choice, and two-ply bounded composition planning. Every trial records `WIN`, `LOSS`, or `UNKNOWN`, plus maximum tray occupancy, actions at occupancy 8–10, score, actions, compositions, and remaining tiles. `UNKNOWN` means the budget ended in a live state; it is never counted as defeat. These policies are probes, not calibrated human players. The offline runner has a 100-second process limit; the client performs no bulk simulation or search.

The committed report is reproducible by regeneration; tests re-evaluate four spaced samples and verify all 105 identities/hashes. If a bank or recognition version changes, regenerate and review rather than silently reusing old difficulty metadata. The UI currently displays only directly measured neutral facts and explicitly says difficulty is unranked. Do not infer Easy/Hard tiers from a constructed witness or raw solver runtime.

## Observed bank results

The full 105-board run took 36.3 seconds and produced a 194,913-byte JSON report. All boards have 90 tiles, four layers, and 19 initially exposed tiles. Whole-board feasible compositions range from 20 to 39 (mean 28.72), ambiguous characters from 8 to 29 (mean 18.64), and competing composition pairs from 20 to 101 (mean 51.43). The certified witness needs at most 3–4 tray slots and never reaches the 8-slot pressure threshold. Those low witness pressures are construction artifacts, not a player-difficulty assessment.

Across one fixed trial per strategy per board, immediate-compose won 0/105, random-legal 0/105, capacity-aware 2/105, and bounded lookahead 0/105. All other trials reached terminal losses; none exhausted the 140-action budget. The two capacity-aware wins were challenge numbers 49 and 81. That sparse signal cannot calibrate human success or establish meaningful tiers. Challenges 57 and 78 have the lowest feasible-composition count (20); challenge 88 has the most competing pairs (101). These are diagnostic outliers, not evidence of easy or hard boards. Repeated human playtests and stronger, validated simulated policies are needed before ranking or gating.

No board showed repeated policy wins, so none is labelled trivial. The near-universal policy losses are a playtest warning about possible frustration, not proof of unfairness: every board still has an engine-replayed winning certificate, and these policies are not human-behavior models.

## Future player progress and ranking

Keep separate records keyed by `(puzzleId, rulesVersion)` for completion, elapsed active time, move count, raw bird-name score, failed attempts, and personal best. A difficulty tier, if validated later, belongs to a versioned collection assignment, not the board identity. A reasonable first gate is tutorial completion for a guided recommendation only, with direct challenge access retained; stricter unlocks require playtest evidence. Same-puzzle personal bests can compare like-for-like attempts. Tier achievements can count certified completions after tiers are validated. Raw scores across different boards are not comparable because character distributions differ. Any cross-puzzle normalization needs empirical score/time distributions and fairness review before a formula or leaderboard is implemented. No accounts, persistence, synthetic player data, or numerical ranking formula are introduced here.
